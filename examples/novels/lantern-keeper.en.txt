Chapter 1: The Storm

The storm came in from the west just after sunset.

Mara climbed the spiral stairs of the old lighthouse two at a time, her red raincoat dripping, a brass lantern swinging from her hand. At the top, the great lamp had gone dark. Wind howled through a cracked pane, and rain lashed the glass so hard it sounded like gravel.

She struck a match, cupped it against the draft, and lit the wick. Light flooded the lantern room and swept out across the black water.

That was when she saw the ship: a three-masted schooner, its sails torn to ribbons, heeling hard toward the rocks below the cliff. A single green light burned on its deck.

Mara grabbed the coil of rope from its hook and ran.

Chapter 2: The Stranger

By the time she reached the beach, the schooner had broken apart on the reef. Waves the size of houses crashed against the shore, and the wreckage burned with a strange blue fire that the rain could not put out.

Someone was clinging to a broken mast in the surf.

Mara tied the rope around her waist, knotted the other end to a rock, and waded in. The cold stole her breath. A wave knocked her flat, but she came up with a fistful of soaked wool and dragged the stranger onto the sand.

He was a young man with silver hair and a long scar across one cheek. In his arms he held a lantern of green glass. Its flame was still burning, small and steady, as if the storm did not exist.

"Don't let it go out," he whispered. Then his eyes closed.

Chapter 3: The Lantern

The stranger slept for two days in the keeper's cottage, wrapped in quilts beside the iron stove.

Mara sat at the kitchen table with the green lantern in front of her. Up close she could see tiny shapes moving inside the flame: a city of white towers, a bridge made of light, a dragon curled around a crescent moon.

On the third morning she woke to find the cottage door wide open. The stranger stood at the edge of the cliff in the pale dawn, holding the lantern high above his head.

The sea below had gone perfectly still. Then, one by one, lights rose from the water, hundreds of them, drifting upward like slow golden fireflies until the whole sky glowed.

"They have been waiting a long time," he said, without turning around. "Thank you for keeping the light on."
